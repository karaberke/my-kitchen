# Apple inspired app demos

My Kitchen's strongest direction is **one understandable everyday problem, resolved through the real app**: butter is missing, olive oil works tonight, and butter stays on the next shopping list. The useful Apple influence is disciplined attention, continuous movement, and a clear payoff. Apple's public guidance supports cohesive app footage, legible content, restrained transitions, and a film that works silently. It does not disclose the production process behind Apple's campaigns. This report combines that guidance with WWDC lectures, perception and learning research, and marketing evidence to develop two completed edits: a complete journey of **42 seconds**, and a focused **32.5-second** alternative. These are creative experiments, not audience-tested winners. ([Apple: App Previews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/), [Apple: Asset Best Practices](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/asset-best-practices/))

## Apple's public guidance favors clarity and continuity

Apple's _Designing Fluid Interfaces_ lecture treats fluidity as more than reaching 60 frames per second: responsiveness and direct manipulation matter. _Animate with springs_ emphasizes continuous position and velocity, including motion that settles without bouncing. For this video, the inference is to use one purposeful swipe, decelerate into a useful reading position, and pause before the next action. There is no need to add decorative bounce or fabricate transitions the app does not have. ([WWDC18: Designing Fluid Interfaces](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/), [WWDC23: Animate with springs](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10158/))

Apple's app-preview guidance recommends capturing the native interface without zooming, allows touch hotspots and straightforward fades or dissolves, and requires transitions to represent functionality honestly. Its asset guidance favors a clear focal point and enough time to understand the visuals. Our interpretation is to keep one phone viewport, cut after meaningful results settle, and use a dissolve only when it clarifies an omission. A dissolve between dense text screens can briefly obscure both; compare it against a clean cut on the actual footage. ([Apple: App Previews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/), [Apple: Asset Best Practices](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/asset-best-practices/))

**App Store previews must be 15–30 seconds**, but that publishing requirement does not govern this general promotional film. A short browser-recorded alternate also does not automatically qualify for App Store submission. ([Apple: App preview specifications](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-preview-specifications/))

## The research supports selective editing, with limits

The following evidence informs editing decisions; none directly tests this My Kitchen film or promises better conversion.

| Evidence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Editing choice for My Kitchen                                                                      | Practical limit                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Smith's attentional account explains how matched action and screen position can preserve expectations across cuts. ([2012 paper](https://eprints.bbk.ac.uk/6679/1/6679.pdf))                                                                                        | Keep the target near its established position; avoid decorative pans and zooms.                    | Film-perception theory, not an experiment comparing app-demo transitions. |
| Zacks and colleagues found that changes in the depicted situation predicted event boundaries; cuts alone did not after accounting for those changes. ([2010 paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2010.00168/full)) | Structure the edit around choosing, noticing, saving, substituting, and finishing.                 | Does not identify an ideal cut frequency or runtime.                      |
| Mayer and Moreno describe removing extraneous material and directing attention to relevant information in multimedia learning. ([2003 paper](https://carpentries.github.io/instructor-training/files/papers/mayer-reduce-cognitive-load-2003.pdf))                  | Remove repetitive navigation and routine field setup; retain the meaningful action and its result. | Educational transfer tests are not advertising-effectiveness tests.       |
| Brysbaert's meta-analysis estimates average adult English nonfiction reading at 238 words per minute, with substantial variation. ([2019 paper](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/8647789))                                                                       | Protect a stationary answer hold, initially about eight seconds, then adjust to the actual text.   | Small UI text and finding the answer add demands beyond ordinary reading. |

At 238 words per minute, 30 words take about 7.6 seconds; at 175, about 10.3 seconds. These are planning calculations, not sufficient viewing times. Count the genuine answer's words, assess its displayed size, and recover time elsewhere before shortening its hold. A faster overall film should contain fewer incidental steps rather than rush its explanation.

## Make one benefit carry the marketing story

Apple's _Presenting Design Work_ recommends a relatable situation, a simply defined problem, and an anticipated payoff. Apple's description of _The Underdogs_ similarly presents people using apps to accomplish a concrete task under pressure. That campaign description supports a task-led premise; this research did not watch or analyze its individual shots. ([WWDC18: Presenting Design Work](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/811/), [Apple: Be an Office Hero](https://apps.apple.com/us/story/id1455561115))

Google's ABCD guide encourages early product presence and a focused, tangible message. Its evidence is explicitly correlational, with results varying by context. Our creative inference is to show the app immediately and let the missing butter provide a modest, recognizable obstacle. The recipe photo supplies appetite; the actual answer supplies reassurance; pantry confirmation supplies proof. No claim of conversion improvement follows from these sources. ([Google: ABCD reference guide](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/youtube_google_abcd_reference_guide_en.pdf))

## Compare a complete routine with a focused rescue

**A — One less thing, 42 seconds.** The film opens on Today with neighboring dinners visible in the populated week. It moves promptly to the pasta photo and missing-butter row, shows enough manual grocery entry to establish intent, then shows butter saved for the next shop. The short question and genuine Ollama answer lead to the essential cooking, olive-oil substitution, pantry confirmation, and a brief settled ending. Actual question typing lasts **5.18 seconds**, and the full unchanged reply remains visible for **8.089 seconds**. All motion plays at its original speed. This is the recommended main film because it preserves the full weekly-planning story.

**B — Dinner rescued, 32.5 seconds.** The alternate begins with the recipe photo and butter shortage. It omits the weekly-plan opening and repetitive cooking details, preserving the same grocery, assistant, substitution, and confirmation logic. This tests a problem-first entry point and smaller story scope. Omitted actions are not presented as automatic. The full week remains populated in the underlying app.

Both use actual footage, the existing photo, the unaltered assistant response, no added captions or narration, and restrained touch cues. The actual master capture is verified at **780 × 1688 and 60 fps**, preserving the complete 390 × 844 CSS-pixel viewport without upscaling. Moving-source PNG captures have a median cadence of approximately **59.81 fps**; the raw footage and PNGs are retained, with no artificial frame interpolation. The new genuine Ollama reply is: “Yes, you can use olive oil instead of butter. Adjust the amount to taste.” It specifies no quantity. The fictional cook records **22 g olive oil as their own choice**, reducing its pantry stock from 400 g to 378 g. All 16 workflow checks pass, including the independent 250 g butter item remaining pending, seven populated days, and decoded recipe imagery. Completed exports run **42.000 seconds** and **32.500 seconds**, respectively, at native 780 × 1688 and 60 fps without resizing. Final visual review is underway; neither film has been audience-tested.

## Choose the edit that survives silent viewing

Compare both at normal phone size, muted first, in alternating order. An unfamiliar viewer should identify the meal, missing ingredient, substitute, future shopping item, and successful pantry update after one viewing. Check that each tap has an understandable result and that the answer can be read without replaying. Ask separately which version feels natural and where attention drifts; a small qualitative review is diagnostic, not a statistical A/B test.

Then compare the restrained music mix. Apple recommends continuity through one score and creative that works silently first. Let sound support a few narrative arrivals and resolve on completion. Select the shorter version only if it retains understanding; the complete journey remains the better main film when the weekly-planning benefit matters. ([Apple: App Previews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/), [Apple: Asset Best Practices](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/asset-best-practices/))
