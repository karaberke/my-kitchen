# App-demo marketing evidence and narrative variants

## What does Apple actually recommend for app-preview films?

### Takeaway

The useful Apple precedent is a coherent demonstration with restrained editing, legible interface content, and an honest depiction of the product. This is public App Store guidance, not a disclosed production manual for Apple's own campaign films.

### Cited Findings

- Apple recommends a cohesive story, with additional previews showing something different. It asks developers to stay inside the app and depict genuine functionality. — [Apple: App Previews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/)
- Apple recommends capturing the native UI rather than zooming, permits touch hotspots, and recommends straightforward dissolves or fades. Transitions must not suggest functionality the app lacks. — [Apple: App Previews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/)
- App Store previews autoplay muted. Apple says readable text needs enough screen time and suggests one musical score to maintain continuity. — [Apple: App Previews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/)
- App Store submission specifications set a minimum of 15 seconds and maximum of 30 seconds. This is a distribution requirement for App Store previews, not a universal duration rule for product videos. — [Apple: App preview specifications](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-preview-specifications/)

### Inferences

- Preserve the user's app-only, portrait, no-caption/no-narration direction. Let the real recipe title, missing-stock message, question, answer, and completion state carry meaning.
- Keep a consistent viewport and trim dead time between completed actions. A dissolve should indicate an editorial jump, not impersonate an app animation.
- Pick an actual recipe-photo frame as the optional poster image. Do not show a blank startup or a partially typed form as the first visible frame.

### Gaps

- These pages do not prescribe a particular easing curve, cut rhythm, or soundtrack tempo for Apple's campaign films. Such choices remain editorial hypotheses.
- The current browser recording is not evidence of native iOS capture. A 30-second alternate alone does not establish App Store submission readiness; device specifications and other disclosure requirements still apply if that destination is later chosen.

## What does original advertising research support, and what is uncertain?

### Takeaway

Focus attention on the product early, then let one understandable problem develop into a visible resolution. Evidence supports testing clarity and engagement separately; it does not prove that a particular duration or more animation will sell this app.

### Cited Findings

- Google's ABCD reference guide recommends introducing the product or brand within five seconds and delivering a focused, tangible message. Its underlying program classified more than 5,000 TrueView in-stream ads using ad recall, consideration, and purchase-intent metrics. Google explicitly describes the guidance as correlational, variable by brand/region/vertical, and not a performance guarantee. This is platform-published marketing evidence, not an independent randomized comparison of these My Kitchen edits. — [Google: ABCD reference guide](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/youtube_google_abcd_reference_guide_en.pdf)
- Teixeira, Wedel, and Pieters measured joy/surprise through facial expressions, attention through eye tracking, and retention through skipping in a controlled video-ad experiment. Their results associate surprise and joy with concentrated attention and retention, with different time dynamics. The public abstract does not validate any exact duration or edit for a silent app demo. — [Journal of Marketing Research, 2012](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.10.0207)
- Pieters, Wedel, and Batra distinguish dense perceptual features from elaboration of creative design. Across 249 advertisements tested with eye tracking, feature complexity hurt brand attention/ad attitude, while design complexity aided pictorial/whole-ad attention, comprehensibility, and attitude. This is not evidence that all visually simple ads outperform all complex ones. The public abstract was accessible; the full paper was restricted. — [Journal of Marketing, 2010](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmkg.74.5.048)

### Inferences

- Treat the butter shortage as a small, relatable obstacle and the substitution plus pantry confirmation as the payoff. No artificial crisis, confetti, or emotional claim is necessary.
- Simplify the edit by omitting routine field setup, backtracking, and repeated scrolling. Do not hide or rewrite product controls to manufacture a cleaner UI.
- Keep the assistant question and real answer at normal reading speed. Recover seconds from navigation and form entry, not from the one moment that explains the solution.
- Preserve the product's name/header where naturally available; do not add a separate logo slate that delays the story.

### Gaps

- No cited study measures this application, audience, recipe, assistant response, or the proposed variants. No conversion uplift can be claimed.
- Google's advice about multiple early shots and people on camera does not automatically transfer to this app-only brief. The user's natural-use direction takes precedence.
- The two academic abstracts support bounded findings; no unreported sample characteristics, effect sizes, or causal claims are inferred.

## Which variants are worth trying, and how should they be judged?

### Takeaway

Produce two deliberately different edits from truthful footage: a complete routine and a focused rescue. A third opening test is useful only if it stays immediately understandable without explanatory captions.

### Cited Findings

- Apple's multiple-preview guidance supports giving each film a distinct purpose; Google's guide encourages experimentation and explicitly rejects a universal creative formula. — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/); [Google](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/youtube_google_abcd_reference_guide_en.pdf)

### Inferences

The following are editorial proposals, not durations established by research. Adjust them to the actual answer's reading length.

| Variant                         | Purpose                               | Proposed rhythm                                                                                                                                                           | Distinctive choice                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A: One less thing, about 42–46s | Main film, full chronological journey | 0–5 weekly plan/today; 5–10 photo and butter shortage; 10–16 manual grocery addition; 16–29 question and real answer; 29–39 cooking/substitute/confirm; 39–42+ completion | Clear cause and effect. One controlled swipe per view, brief settling pauses, greatest time allocation to the answer.                                                                     |
| B: Dinner rescued, about 30–34s | Optional focused alternate            | 0–4 recipe photo and missing butter; 4–8 manually add butter; 8–21 question and real answer; 21–28 substitute/confirm; 28–30+ completion                                  | Starts with the practical obstacle. Omits the weekly-plan scene and repetitive cooking details instead of squeezing the whole story faster. The underlying seeded week remains populated. |
| C: Appetite first, about 42–46s | Opening experiment for the main film  | A 1–2s real recipe-photo/title shot, then weekly plan and the chronological A sequence                                                                                    | Food appeal appears immediately. Keep only if a silent viewer understands the brief initial image as editorial setup, not unexplained navigation. Otherwise use A.                        |

Common constraints for every rendered option: actual running app, genuine unedited Ollama answer, existing recipe photo, one viewport, no captions/narration, no invented product transitions. The manual butter item must remain pending after cooking; the recorded cooking event must use 30g olive oil and the pantry confirmation must be real. A shorter alternate can omit evidence from the screen, but must not claim omitted steps happened automatically.

Recommended evaluation, before selecting a winner:

1. Watch once muted at a normal phone display size. Can an unfamiliar viewer name tonight's meal, the missing ingredient, and what replaced it?
2. After the assistant moment, can the viewer explain the answer without replaying? If not, extend that hold and trim navigation elsewhere.
3. Can the viewer distinguish adding butter for a future shop from recording olive oil used now? Confusion here is a failed story even if the motion looks attractive.
4. Is each visible state change clearly caused by a tap, normal scroll, or obvious editorial cut? Remove unexplained jumps and cut while the screen is settled.
5. Does the finish read as completed cooking with a confirmed pantry update, rather than merely closing the assistant?
6. Compare A and B in randomized order with otherwise identical audio/export quality. Ask which feels more natural, which is easier to follow, and where attention drifted. Small qualitative feedback is diagnostic, not a statistically established winner.

### Gaps

- No audience test has been performed in this research subtask. These are proposed questions, not claimed findings.
- The ideal assistant hold depends on the actual response, its visible line count, and viewing size. A shorter runtime should be abandoned if it damages reading comprehension.
